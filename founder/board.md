# Board summary: Schleswig Bike Share (working name)

## The vote

**2 FUND IF, 1 PASS**. Average score **3.7 / 10** (Offers 3 PASS, Monopoly 4 FUND IF, Product 4 FUND IF).

Memos: [offers](board/offers.md) · [monopoly](board/monopoly.md) · [product](board/product.md)

## Risks more than one member raised

1. **10 bikes spread across a town means "no bike near me" (all three).** Bike sharing only works when a bike is close by. If a rider walks to an empty spot once or twice, they cancel, and the network never gets dense enough to matter.
2. **The real competition is what young people already have (all three).** Their own bike, walking, the bus and school transport. Nobody has shown that any of these hurts enough to make people pay. Once a pupil owns a bike, the reason to subscribe is gone.
3. **Half the revenue is unconfirmed (all three).** The city's contribution and the ad income have no amount, no terms and no signature. The pilot has to work on rides and subscriptions alone until they are signed.
4. **The hidden operations are blank (Offers, Product).** Maintenance, rebalancing, theft, damage, insurance, app and lock hardware: none has an owner or a cost.
5. **Weak market (Offers, Monopoly).** Young people and pupils have the least money to spend, and the target group has not been counted.

## Where they disagree

- **The Offers lens passes** because the market itself is weak: low spending power and an unproven pain. Better execution cannot fix a bad market.
- **The Monopoly lens sees a way in**: one small town is a market you could own, and the city, which controls street space, could grant exclusivity. That would be the only durable advantage.
- **The Product lens says the idea is fixable** if it shrinks to one trip done brilliantly, not a town covered thinly.

## Conditions checklist

- [ ] Count the target group and prove a frequent trip that hurts, e.g. school ↔ station (`/founder-consumer`)
- [ ] Map substitutes and any existing or announced operators in Schleswig; confirm nobody already covers the town (`/founder-competitors`)
- [ ] Narrow the pilot to one route or zone and one core trip; set a maximum walk distance to a bike and measure it
- [ ] Guarantee bike availability for that core trip
- [ ] Make the pilot pay on rides and subscriptions alone; treat city money and ads as upside until signed (`/founder-cfo`)
- [ ] Cost per bike per month including theft, damage, maintenance, rebalancing, insurance, app and locks; rides or subscriptions per day needed to break even (`/founder-cfo`, `/founder-ops`)
- [ ] A written city agreement: operator status, exclusivity or first right for a set number of years, funding amount and terms
- [ ] A signed pilot with at least one school that brings in pupil subscribers without paid ads
- [ ] Confirm pupils will actually pay 5 € a month, and solve sign-up and payment for under-18s, including parental consent (`/founder-consumer`)
- [ ] One-page map of a ride from download to return, with a named owner for unlocking, repairs, rebalancing and support (`/founder-ops`)
- [ ] A costed plan for growing past 10 bikes, and the pilot numbers that would trigger it (`/founder-cfo`)
- [ ] Real sign-ups at the stated prices before buying bikes (`/founder-launch`)

## The strongest version the board can see

This is not "bike sharing for Schleswig". It is a **school-commute service run as the city's official partner**. Put all 10 bikes on one painful route (for example, between the train station and the schools) so a bike is always there at 7:30 and 13:00. Sell it to schools and the city as a mobility service with pupil subscriptions, not as a commodity rental competing with the bike a pupil already owns. Get the city agreement, including exclusivity and funding, in writing before buying bikes, and let ads come later as a bonus. This is narrower than what you pitched, and the board thinks that is exactly why it could work.
