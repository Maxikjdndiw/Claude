# Buyer panel: Schleswig Bike Share (working name): app-based bike sharing, 10-bike pilot

**2 buy · 18 pass** (10% buy) out of 20 simulated buyers. Seed 492621, so the same cards can be dealt again.

These are simulated buyers, not customers. Use this to find objections and weak spots, then confirm the big ones with real people before you spend.

## What would flip a no

- If my own bike got stolen or broken, and I could pay per ride without a card my parents have to set up, I'd try a few rides.
- If it's spring, my bike is still broken and the first rides are free so I can see whether a bike is actually near my school.
- If I could pay myself without my parents (like PayPal, a prepaid card or a school pass) and there was always a bike at my school or the station.
- A bike always waiting at the bus station, and a way to pay without my parents' card, like topping up with cash or a voucher.
- If my bike got stolen or broke, I might pay 1.50 for a few rides until it's fixed, but only if a bike is actually near me when I need it.
- If my own bike got broken or stolen and a bike was reliably parked at the train station in the morning, I'd pay per ride for a while.
- A few bikes always parked at the train station, ideally one I can reserve in the app before I get off the train, and the 5 euro rate for apprentices too.
- A bike I could reliably pick up at the bus stop and ride out to my village and leave there, paid with cash or a prepaid card without my parents.
- If my own bike broke or got stolen and there was a bike reliably parked near my home and near the training hall, I'd try the 5 € pupil month.
- Seeing in the app that there are bikes parked near my place and my school most of the time, so it's actually faster than walking when I'm late.
- Bikes reliably parked at the bus stops in town, so I could ride the last bit when my own bike is broken or stolen.
- If my own bike broke or got stolen and I needed to get somewhere quickly, I might pay 1.50 € for a single ride.

Buyers say they would buy **2.0 times** in the first month on average.
