# Monopoly lens
Score: 4 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The market is small, so it could be owned.** One town and one age group is the kind of narrow starting market the Monopoly lens likes. If nobody else rents bikes by app in Schleswig, a 10-bike pilot could start with close to 100% of a small market.
- **The city could be a moat.** A city that pays part of the running costs is also the body that grants street space. An exclusive or preferred-operator agreement would be the one durable advantage this business could realistically hold. The other three are weak here: it has no proprietary technology, any network effect only works inside one town, and it has no scale.
- **There is a distribution channel.** Pupils are gathered in schools, so the first 100 customers could come through one institution rather than paid ads. The pupil plan (5 €, the price of about 3.3 single rides) makes that pitch easy.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Nothing stops a copycat.** Bike sharing is an off-the-shelf product with no secret. An established operator or a city-run scheme could arrive in month six with more bikes and an app that already works, and 10 bikes cannot match that. Without an exclusive city agreement, the business has no lasting advantage.
2. **10 bikes may be too few to be useful.** Bike sharing only works when a bike is nearby, so a town with 10 bikes can feel empty. If riders cannot find a bike, they stop subscribing, and the network never grows to the density that would make it hard to copy.
3. **Its "competitors" are mostly things people already own.** Young people in a small town may already own a bike, walk, or take the bus. The real competition is probably substitutes, not other operators, and the brief does not say whether any of them are worse enough to make people switch.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What do you know about Schleswig, its schools or its city hall that an outside operator does not? (the secret)
- Who already offers bike or e-scooter rental, bus passes or school transport within reach of these riders? Answered by **/founder-competitors**.
- Will the city give you exclusivity, for how long, and how much will it pay? Answered by a conversation with the city, then priced by **/founder-cfo**.
- How many pupils and young adults would actually subscribe instead of using their own bike? Answered by **/founder-consumer**.
- What does each bike cost per month after theft, damage, maintenance and rebalancing, and how many subscriptions cover that? Answered by **/founder-cfo**.
- Why now? What has changed so that this works in Schleswig today?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. A written agreement with the city that names you the operator, with exclusivity or first right for a set number of years, plus the funding amount and terms.
2. **/founder-competitors** confirms that no operator already covers Schleswig or has announced plans to.
3. A plan, with a cost checked by **/founder-cfo**, for how the fleet grows from 10 bikes to enough to be useful, and the pilot numbers that would trigger it.
4. A signed pilot with at least one school that gets pupil subscribers without paid advertising.
