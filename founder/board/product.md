# Product lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The one-sentence pitch nearly exists: "unlock a bike with your phone, 1.50 € a ride, 5 € a month if you are a pupil." That is clear.
- A defined user (young people, pupils) gives you someone to design the first five minutes around.
- The pricing is simple. 12 € a month equals 8 rides at 1.50 €; 5 € equals about 3.3 rides. A regular rider can see the choice in seconds.
- A 10-bike pilot is a small, cheap way to learn before committing. That is the right instinct.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The experience fails at the first moment: there is no bike near me.** With 10 bikes across a whole town, the likely first five minutes is opening the app, finding the nearest bike too far away or already taken, and walking. Bike sharing only works when a bike is there when you need it. Nothing in the brief says where the 10 bikes will be, how they get moved back, or who checks on them. How many bikes the target area needs is an open question (/founder-ops, /founder-consumer).
2. **No focus: four revenue streams and no core.** Rides, subscriptions, ads and a city subsidy each pull the product a different way. Advertisers want visibility, the city wants coverage or social goals, riders want a working bike right now. A 10-bike pilot cannot serve all of them. Ad income and city money are unknown (/founder-cfo), so they should not shape the design yet.
3. **The parts nobody sees are blank.** Maintenance, flat tyres, theft, damage, insurance, app build or licence, unlock hardware: none of these have an owner or a cost. In this product, a broken bike or a lock that won't open is the moment the customer remembers. Who owns each of these, and what it costs, is an open question (/founder-ops, /founder-cfo).

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Describe one pupil's ride from start to finish: where they open the app, how far they walk, how they unlock, where they leave the bike, and what happens if the lock fails.
- What is the one trip this must handle brilliantly (school run, station to town, evenings out)? Put all 10 bikes there.
- Are you building the app and locks yourself or licensing them? Who answers when something breaks at 7:40 on a school morning?
- How do pupils under 18 pay and sign up? Is parental consent needed? (open question: /founder-consumer)
- What would you cut if the city pays nothing and no advertiser signs?
- What do young people in Schleswig use today instead: own bikes, buses, walking, e-scooters? (/founder-competitors)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Narrow the pilot to one route or zone and one core trip, dense enough that a rider usually finds a bike close by. Write the target walk distance down and measure it.
2. Write a one-page map of the ride from first download to bike returned, with a named owner for unlock, repair, rebalancing and support.
3. Make the pilot work on ride and subscription revenue alone. Treat ads and the city contribution as upside until they are signed, with the numbers checked by /founder-cfo.
4. Run /founder-consumer to confirm pupils will actually use it at 5 € a month, and /founder-competitors to check the substitutes, before buying bikes.
