# Offers lens
Score: 3/10 | Vote: PASS

## What is strong
- A cheap pilot (10 bikes) means a failed test costs little and can teach a lot.
- The price ladder is simple and easy to grasp. 12 € a month pays for itself at 8 rides (12 / 1.50), and the 5 € pupil plan pays off from the 4th ride (5 / 1.50 = 3.3). For a regular rider that is easy to sell.
- Low effort on paper: find the bike in the app, unlock it, ride. Effort is the term this business could win on.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The market fails the market test.** The buyers are young people and pupils. They have the least money to spend, and the founder already discounts them to 5 €. The pain is unproven: in a small town, a young person can walk, ride their own bike or take the bus. Nobody has shown the pain is big. The brief gives no count of target customers and no sign the market is growing. Weak market plus low spending power is the most common reason this lens passes.
2. **It competes on price alone.** As described, the only thing on offer is "a bike, cheaply". There is no stronger outcome, no guarantee and no stack. Once a pupil owns a bike, the reason to pay is gone. That is a commodity.
3. **Likelihood fails on day one.** With 10 bikes spread across a city, the chance a bike is near you when you need it looks low. The founder has not said whether bikes return to fixed stations. A subscriber who walks to an empty spot twice will cancel. Theft, damage and who maintains the bikes are all unknown, and each one cuts likelihood further. Half the revenue model (city money, ad money) is also unconfirmed. So the offer has to stand on rides alone, and it has not been shown that it can.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Who exactly has this pain? How many pupils and young adults in Schleswig lack a bike or a good bus route for a trip they make every week? (/founder-consumer)
- Who already meets this need: buses, school transport, bike rental, people's own bikes? (/founder-competitors)
- How far would a rider walk to reach a bike, and how often would 10 bikes leave them stranded?
- What would make a pupil feel stupid saying no? A guaranteed bike near the school at set times? A ride-home guarantee?
- Has the city committed money in writing, how much, and for how long? Has any advertiser named a price? (/founder-cfo once known)
- What does a bike cost, and the app, and maintenance, theft and insurance? How many rides a day per bike to break even? (/founder-cfo)
- Could you charge double, and would anyone still choose this over their own bike?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
Not applicable (PASS). To reach FUND IF, the founder would need to:
- show a specific group with a frequent trip that hurts (for example school to station);
- guarantee bike availability for that trip;
- show real sign-ups at the stated prices;
- get the city contribution confirmed in writing.
